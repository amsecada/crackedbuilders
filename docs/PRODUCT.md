# Product Definition

## Product

Adam Secada personal website.

This is a single-page personal brand and builder portfolio.

It exists primarily for people who encounter Adam through social media, professional networks, communities, projects, or word of mouth and want to quickly understand who he is.

---

## Primary Goal

Turn curiosity about Adam into:

- conversations
- collaborations
- business opportunities
- relationships with other builders
- interest in his projects and ideas

The website should make someone think:

> This guy builds interesting things, has strong opinions, and is probably worth talking to.

---

## Audience

Primary audiences include:

- founders
- builders
- technologists
- small business operators
- investors
- creators
- AI practitioners
- interesting people working on unusual projects

The site does not need to appeal to everyone.

Some personality and opinion are desirable.

---

## Positioning

Adam sits at the intersection of:

- AI
- technology
- entrepreneurship
- small business
- systems
- experimentation
- building

The website should emphasize builder/operator identity over traditional consultant identity.

---

## Product Principles

### 1. Personal before corporate

The site represents a person, not a company.

Avoid corporate language and generic business clichés.

---

### 2. Show rather than explain

Projects, experiments, writing, and artifacts should demonstrate interests and capability.

Avoid long claims about expertise when the work can make the point instead.

---

### 3. Curiosity beats completeness

The website does not need to explain Adam's entire career.

It should provide enough signal that interesting visitors want to continue the conversation.

---

### 4. Distinctive beats familiar

The website should not look like a standard startup template.

Strong typography and composition are more important than familiar SaaS patterns.

---

### 5. Minimal beats comprehensive

Every section should earn its place.

Avoid unnecessary pages, features, navigation layers, and UI components.

---

## Information Architecture

The site is rendered as a single page.

### Navigation

Persistent or immediately accessible navigation linking to page anchors.

Expected destinations:

- About
- Work
- Writing
- Contact

The Adam Secada wordmark/name may return to the top.

---

## Hero

The hero should immediately establish:

- Adam's name
- builder/operator identity
- short positioning statement
- primary call to action

The dominant visual element should be typography.

A diagonal slash or related geometric motif may become part of the composition.

No portrait is planned.

---

## About

A concise introduction.

This should not be a traditional biography.

It should explain enough about Adam's background and current interests to create context for the work below.

Prefer approximately 1-3 short paragraphs.

---

## Work / Projects

Show selected projects and experiments.

Not every project should receive equal visual weight.

Important projects may receive:

- large typography
- screenshots
- descriptions
- project status
- links

Smaller experiments may appear as compact text entries.

This section should feel like a builder's workbench rather than a corporate case-study library.

---

## Writing / Thinking

Surface selected ideas, essays, posts, notes, or other public thinking.

This section can initially be lightweight.

The purpose is to show what Adam thinks about, not to recreate a publishing platform.

External links are acceptable.

---

## Contact

This is the primary conversion point.

Primary intent:

> Build something interesting? Ping me.

Provide a clear way to contact Adam.

Social links may appear alongside the primary CTA.

---

## Footer

Minimal.

Potential information:

- Adam Secada
- current year
- selected social links
- small visual or textual signature

Avoid a large conventional sitemap.

---

## Initial Release Scope

Version 1 should contain real content for:

- navigation
- hero
- about
- contact

Work and writing sections may initially use partially complete content.

The first release exists to establish:

- design language
- typography
- responsive system
- core personality
- page structure

Do not delay the initial implementation while attempting to create a complete historical portfolio.

---

## Non-Goals

Version 1 does not require:

- CMS
- database
- authentication
- blog engine
- newsletter infrastructure
- analytics dashboard
- multiple pages
- search
- user accounts
- complex animations
- backend services

These may be reconsidered later.

---

## Success Criteria

The website succeeds if a visitor can understand within roughly 30 seconds:

1. who Adam is
2. what kinds of things he builds
3. what subjects interest him
4. how to contact him

And the presentation should be memorable enough that it does not blend into the sea of generic founder and developer portfolios.
